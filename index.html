What we're building

npc-minds: generative agents that remember, plan and talk to each other

An engine that gives every character in a simulated world its own mind, after Stanford's Generative Agents research (Park et al., 2023). Each agent keeps a memory stream, reflects on what it has lived through, plans its day, and passes news to the agents it meets - so information spreads through a population the way it does through a town.

  • Lives, not just characters. Every agent is born from an archetype with sampled Big Five personality traits; when it dies, the slot is reborn as someone new. A ledger keeps every life for study.
  • Built for research. Interviews that write nothing back, a grounding check that flags any claim an agent's memories don't support, information-diffusion experiments, relationship networks and trait-versus-outcome statistics.
  • Private by design. Text a human typed never leaves the machine - enforced in code and proven by a test - while the agents' own thinking can use hosted models.
  • Runs on almost nothing. A cascade across many free hosted models, batching to stretch every quota, and a small local model as the last resort, so a population keeps thinking at near-zero cost.

Research methodology

Measure the artifact, not the exit code

AI systems fail quietly: a pipeline reports success while the output is wrong. Our method treats every result as unproven until it has been measured - the discipline we apply to software, and now to energy research.

Verify outputs directly

Every stage is checked against what it produced, not what it reported. A green checkmark is not evidence; a measured file, frame or reading is.

Calibrated AI judgement

AI evaluators get forced-choice questions instead of yes/no (which invites agreement bias), and every automated check carries its own measured noise floor, so a warning means something.

Applied to energy

For wireless power, AI-assisted modelling explores the design space - frequency band, path loss through real materials, rectifier efficiency, beam-steering control - and every model is held to bench measurements.

Flagship R&D

Compact wireless power for battery-operated devices Patent pending

Inductive charging only works when a device sits on a pad. EnergenAI's system is designed to deliver power across a room: a phased-array transmitter forms steerable millimeter-wave beams, and a compact receiver inside the device converts them back to regulated DC.

  • Beam-steered delivery. An electronically steerable phased array directs energy at authenticated receivers and follows them as they move.
  • AI-driven control. The control system tracks devices and continuously tunes beam direction, frequency and power for efficiency and safety.
  • Safety by design. Designed around FCC and ICNIRP exposure guidance, with thermal management and frequency hopping to avoid interference.
  • Built to embed. A compact receiver suited to phones, IoT sensors, wearables and smart-home devices.
